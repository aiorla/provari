# The proof format

Version 1. Files in it end in `.pvr`.

A proof shows that one side, the attacker, can force a win from its root:
the side to move, unless the header names the other side. It holds only the
attacker's moves, in the order a walk through the proof meets them; the
defences are not written but generated by the rules, so a checker rebuilds
every position itself, and where the attacker wins within two moves by a
check or at once, the checker finds the win itself and nothing is written.
Whatever a file holds, a proof the checker accepts is a forced win: a wrong
or damaged file can only be rejected.

## Rules

Proofs follow Lichess's Atomic rules, with these conditions:

- **The 50-move rule**: a position whose 50-move counter has reached 100
  must already be over.
- **No repetition**: no line may repeat a position. Positions are compared
  by their board, the side to move, the castling rights and the en passant
  square, the last only when an en passant capture is legal, but not by
  their counters.
- **Tablebases**: if the header allows them, a position with at most that
  many pieces, its counter at 0 (as after a capture or pawn move) and
  without castling rights, is decided by the Atomic Syzygy tables: a win for
  the attacker to move, or a loss for the defender to move, ends the line as
  won. Any other value decides nothing.

A line is won when the defender's king explodes or the defender is mated,
or by the tablebases as above. It fails if the game ends otherwise (the
attacker's king exploded, a stalemate, insufficient material), at the
50-move limit, or by a repetition.

## The order of moves

Moves are numbered in an order that does not depend on any program:

1. the moves after which the opponent is in check come first: its king is
   attacked by one of the mover's pieces and the two kings are not on
   adjacent squares (a side without a king is not in check);
2. within each group, by the square a move leaves, then the square it
   reaches, then its promotion (none, knight, bishop, rook, queen). Squares
   count from a1 = 0, b1 = 1, ... to h8 = 63. Castling is the king's move two
   squares towards its rook, and en passant the pawn's move to the en passant
   square.

The attacker's moves are written as their index in this order among the
position's legal moves. The walk takes the defences by the second rule
alone, in square order, as their order only sets the order of the walk.

## Layout

A proof is one line of JSON, the header, ending with a newline, then a zstd
frame holding the moves and nothing after it (`provari` writes the frame
with zstd's checksum, so that a damaged file shows as damaged):

```json
{"provari":1,"variant":"atomic","root":"FEN","tablebases":5,"moves":28,"positions":2361}
```

- `provari`: the format's version, 1.
- `variant`: `atomic`.
- `root`: the position proven, in FEN; its 50-move counter counts, its move
  number does not. It may hold more material than a game can reach, as
  Lichess allows.
- `attacker`: `white` or `black`, only where the attacker is not the side to
  move at the root; the walk then starts with the defender's moves.
- `tablebases`: the most pieces a tablebase verdict may have, 0 for none
  (`provari` writes the most pieces of a position whose verdict ends a
  line, whatever tables it had, so 0 where none does). A checker without
  the tables up to that many pieces cannot check the proof.
- `moves`: the proof's length, the most attacker moves any line takes; a
  tablebase win counts as won where it is reached.
- `positions`: the number of moves written.

The header has exactly these fields, `attacker` only where it is needed, so
that a proof has one header. The frame's content is the moves, each an
unsigned LEB128 number in its shortest form: one byte for an index below
128, two above (never met in practice). It holds exactly `positions`
numbers.

## The walk

The checker walks the proof depth first from the root, reading a move where
it needs one; the writer writes them in the same order.

```text
check(root):
    if root is over: it must be won
    else if the attacker is to move: attacker(root)
    else defender(root)
    all moves read, nothing left over, and no repetition (below)

attacker(X):                      # the attacker to move; X not over, counter < 100
    if X's position is proven at a counter at least X's: done
    if X is settled (below): X is proven
    else:
        i = next move; it must be below the number of X's legal moves
        Y = X after its move i
        defender(Y)
        X is proven
    record X's position as proven at X's counter, if higher than before

defender(Y):                      # the defender to move
    if Y is over: it must be won, done
    if Y's counter is 100 or more: fail
    if the tablebases lose Y: done
    for each legal move of Y in square order, reaching X:
        if X is over, or X's counter is 100 or more: fail
        attacker(X)
```

An attacker position is **settled**, with nothing written for it, when:

1. the tablebases win it; or
2. a move wins at once: it explodes the defender's king or mates; or
3. a check wins in two: taking the moves that give check in order, the
   first after which the position reached is lost for the defender by the
   tablebases, or every defence reaches a position that the tablebases win
   or where a capture wins at once. A check qualifies only if neither the
   position it reaches nor any its defences reach is over or at the 50-move
   limit.

A position proven at some counter is proven at any lower one: every line from
it meets the same positions with counters no higher, and tablebase verdicts
only count at a counter of 0, after a capture or pawn move, as before.

**Repetition**: link each written attacker position to the position its move
reaches, and each defender position to every position its moves reach. The
proof is valid only if these links, between positions compared without
their counters, form no cycle. The positions inside a settled win need no
links: one equal to an earlier position of its line would have settled that
position too, and one reached by a capture or pawn move cannot equal an
earlier position at all.

**Length**: the most attacker moves a line takes. An attacker position won
by the tablebases counts 0, one with a move that wins at once 1, and one
with a written move, or settled by its first qualifying check, 1 plus the
most that a position its defences reach counts; a defender position over or
lost by the tablebases counts 0, and any other the most that a position its
moves reach counts. A position proven earlier keeps what it was proven
with. The root's length must equal the header's `moves`.

**Counter**: the 50-move counter up to which the root's proof holds, which
`provari check` reports. Only the positions before a line's first capture
or pawn move take their counters from the root's, and none of them can
equal a position after one, so with the root at any counter from its own up
to that one, the walk reads the same moves and settles the same positions
the same way. An attacker position won by the tablebases allows 0, one with
a move that wins at once 99, and any other what its written move or first
qualifying check allows; a defender position over allows 99, one lost by
the tablebases 0, and any other the least that its moves allow. A capture
or pawn move allows 99, and any other move one less than the position it
reaches. A position proven earlier keeps what it was proven with.

## Checking

A checker rejects anything malformed: a header that is not this JSON, a
root that is not a legal position, a zstd frame that is damaged or holds
more or fewer moves than `positions`, an index that is not below the number
of legal moves, and data after the frame. It may refuse proofs above its own
limits on size, positions, depth or time. `provari check` also reads proofs
in JSON lines, which `provari convert` writes in this format.
